The Scale of the Challenge

Waste Dynamics & Urgent Needs

In India alone, approximately 62 million tonnes of municipal solid waste is generated every year. Rapid urbanization and changing consumption patterns are projected to escalate this figure to 165 million tonnes by 2030.

Segregation at the source by individuals is the single most critical factor determining whether materials can be treated or end up in hazardous open landfills.

Holistic Framework

The Extended Waste Management Hierarchy

Moving beyond the basic 3Rs into a comprehensive lifecycle hierarchy ranked from most preferred to least preferred.

Ranked Hierarchy: Most Preferred (Top) to Least Preferred (Bottom)
1

Prevention (Most Preferred)

Stop waste before it exists. Choose digital over print, avoid single-use packaging, and invest in durable, repairable goods.

2

Minimisation

When prevention isn't possible, reduce volume or toxicity by buying in bulk, choosing concentrated cleaners, and opting for non-toxic materials.

3

Reuse

Extend product lifespans before discarding: repurpose glass jars for food storage, convert old textiles into rags, and mend furniture.

4

Recycling

Reprocess clean materials (metals, PET bottles, clean cardboard) into new goods, diverting them from landfills and saving raw materials.

5

Energy Recovery

Extract usable energy (biogas from organic waste or waste-to-energy plants) from items that cannot be mechanically recycled.

6

Disposal (Least Preferred)

Landfilling or incineration without energy recovery. Responsible for methane emissions and leachate; must be minimized at all costs.

Bio-Waste & Organics

Compostable

Food scraps, garden cuttings, vegetable peelings, and compostable solids.

  • Separate food and garden waste from all plastic or general trash.
  • Use aerated compost bins or bokashi systems for rapid decomposition.
  • Never mix with plastic bags, stickers, or metal twist-ties.
  • Use mature compost as organic fertilizer for gardens and houseplants.
🧴

Plastic Waste

Recyclable (PET/HDPE)

Beverage containers, detergent jugs, clean packaging, and hard plastics.

  • Rinse out remaining residues and allow items to dry.
  • Check resin codes: Type 1 (PET) & Type 2 (HDPE) have the highest recycling value.
  • Avoid single-use checkout bags and unrecyclable multi-layer pouches.
  • Drop off rigid plastics in curbside dry recycling bins.
💻

Electronic Waste (E-Waste)

Hazardous / Special

Old smartphones, chargers, laptops, circuit boards, and appliances.

  • Never throw e-waste or batteries into regular household garbage.
  • Factory-reset devices and wipe personal data prior to drop-off.
  • Explore repair, refurbished trade-in, or donation before recycling.
  • Hand over only to authorized, certified e-waste collection centers.
🥫

Metal Waste

Infinitely Recyclable

Aluminium soda cans, tin food cans, foil, scrap metal, and hardware.

  • Rinse food residue from soup cans and beverage tins.
  • Leave pull-tabs attached to aluminium cans to prevent littering.
  • Separate ferrous (magnetic) from non-ferrous metals if required locally.
  • Drop off at dry recycling or take larger scrap to local buyback yards.
🫙

Glass Waste

100% Recyclable

Condiment jars, beverage bottles, olive oil bottles, and glass vessels.

  • Rinse clean to eliminate odor and insect attraction.
  • Remove metal lids and recycle them in the metal bin.
  • Never mix window panes, mirrors, pyrex, or ceramics with bottle glass.
  • Place intact jars into color-segregated or designated glass collection bins.
⚠️

Hazardous & Chemical Waste

Special Depots

Paint cans, pesticides, expired medicines, fluorescent tubes, and solvents.

  • Never pour toxic chemicals, paint, or used motor oil down household drains.
  • Keep chemicals in original labeled containers to prevent dangerous reactions.
  • Return expired prescription medicines to authorized pharmacy drop-boxes.
  • Deliver paints and solvents to designated municipal hazardous waste drives.
